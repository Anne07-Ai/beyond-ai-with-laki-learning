What exactly is a token?

The small pieces of text that language models read and write.

One-minute takeaway: Language models don't read text word by word the way we do. They work with tokens: small pieces of text that can be a whole word, part of a word or a punctuation mark. Tokens are the building blocks that language models use to process and generate text.

1. Think of building blocks

Imagine building something with Lego blocks. An AI model doesn't handle a whole sentence in one go. It works with smaller pieces of text, and those pieces are called tokens. This is a simplified analogy to make the idea easier to picture.

2. An example

A tokenizer might split “I love chocolate” into three tokens:

I ␣love ␣chocolate

The ␣ marks a space. Many tokenizers keep the space in front of a word as part of the token.

3. One token isn't always one word

Here's the surprising part:

  • A long or less common word can be split into smaller, more common pieces. For example, annoyingly might become annoying + ly.
  • Punctuation such as ? or . can be a token of its own.
  • The exact split depends on the model's tokenizer. Different models can split the same text differently.
Note: The splits on this page are illustrative. Real splits depend on the tokenizer. Inside the model, each token is stored as a number, not as letters.

4. Why does this matter?

Language models process tokens, not words exactly as we see them. When you ask ChatGPT a question, your text is converted into tokens. The model uses those tokens and the conversation so far to predict what comes next. It keeps generating tokens, one at a time, until it forms a response.

Your text → Tokens → Context → Next token → Repeat → Reply

Token counts are not word counts. Long words can become several tokens and punctuation can be its own token, so a sentence often has more tokens than words.

5. The context window: the model's desk

Every model has a context window: a limit on how many tokens it can work with at once.

Think of it like a desk. A bigger desk can hold more books and notes while you're working. A bigger context window can hold more information for the model to consider. The size varies from model to model.

Quick quiz

  1. Is one token always one word?
    Answer: No. A token can be a whole word, part of a word or a punctuation mark.
  2. Who decides how text is split into tokens?
    Answer: The model's tokenizer. Different models can split the same text differently.
  3. What is a context window?
    Answer: The limit on how many tokens a model can work with at once, like the size of a desk.

Key words

Token: A piece of text a model works with: a word, part of a word or punctuation. Tokenizer: The model's method for splitting text into tokens. Subword: A piece of a longer or rarer word. Context window: The maximum number of tokens a model can take into account at once.
